\begin{abstract}
We introduce \method, a conditional diffusion framework for few-shot cross-script font generation. It separates two information sources that prior systems entangle.
The task is underdetermined because Chinese references reveal a typeface's appearance but do not directly reveal how its Latin, Japanese, or phonetic glyphs should be designed.
Our \emph{Set-Delta Variation Prior} retains same-character donor glyphs as normalized geometric residuals from a fixed neutral anchor until location-dependent aggregation, while \emph{Graphics-Informed Local Reference Attention} uses cross-script geometric primitives as keys and learned local reference features as values.
Together, these modules make ambiguous cross-script completion a controllable design process: the bank supplies a structured variation space, and the references select the appearance that best represents the target family.
On \tbd{dataset and evaluation protocol}, \method improves \tbd{headline family/style result} while preserving glyph identity at \tbd{identity result}; controlled studies show \tbd{Set-Delta and Graphics-Ref findings}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Designing a font across writing systems is not ordinary image translation.
Given a small set of Chinese glyphs from an unseen typeface, a model may observe weight, slant, contrast, terminal shape, and texture, yet it does not observe the target Latin letter or Japanese kana itself.
Multiple target glyphs can therefore be compatible with the same references.
A generator can exploit this ambiguity by combining a broad vocabulary of proven glyph structures with the target family's visible design language.

Existing few-shot font generators typically combine a neutral content glyph with a global or local style representation~\citep{yang2023fontdiffuser,tang2022fsfont,pan2023vqfont}.
Content-fusion methods additionally blend corresponding characters from basis fonts~\citep{wang2023cffont}.
Two failure modes remain exposed in the cross-script setting.
First, compressing retrieved same-character examples into one mean feature can cancel distinct structural modes and can transmit donor-specific appearance as if it were target-font evidence.
Second, globally pooling a few reference glyphs removes localized cues such as terminals, ligatures, stroke contrast, and dry-brush effects before the generator can use them.

\method addresses the two failure modes with an explicit evidence decomposition (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
The bank answers \emph{what structural variations are plausible for the requested character}; the observed references answer \emph{which visible appearance is compatible with the target family}.
The denoiser combines both sources while the neutral content glyph remains responsible for character identity.

  \label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

The design principle is simple: the font bank expands structural choice, and target references personalize appearance.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We establish an evidence architecture for cross-script font completion: \emph{bank-supported structural variation} defines the design space, while \emph{reference-observed appearance} personalizes the result.
  \item We introduce \setdelta, which preserves normalized, anchor-relative same-character candidates until spatial aggregation, allowing each glyph region to select the most useful structural proposal.
  \item We introduce \graphicsref, which uses graphics primitives to find correspondence and learned reference features to deliver local appearance, retaining terminals, contrast, and brush texture.
  \item We deliver a multidimensional evaluation suite spanning identity, geometry, local style, family compatibility, and perceptual quality, with attributes organized by what the inputs reveal.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Cross-script font transfer.}
FTransGAN transfers a few observed glyphs across languages with multi-level attention~\citep{li2021ftransgan}, and FCAGAN combines full-domain convolutional attention with a multi-layer perceptual discriminator~\citep{zhao2024fcagan}.
DRA-font studies cross-lingual synthesis through diffusion-regularized adversarial learning and enhanced attention~\citep{wu2026drafont}.
Unlike these direct transfer systems, HR-Font casts cross-script generation as bank-supported completion guided by observed reference appearance.

\paragraph{Few-shot font generation.}
FontDiffuser casts one-shot font generation as conditional denoising and introduces multi-scale content aggregation and style contrastive refinement~\citep{yang2023fontdiffuser}.
DG-Font uses deformable feature transport to preserve structure under unsupervised font translation~\citep{xie2021dgfont}.
FSFont learns spatial correspondence between content and fine-grained local styles through cross-attention~\citep{tang2022fsfont}, while VQ-Font combines similarity-guided global style with quantized local components~\citep{pan2023vqfont}.
Local style and spatial alignment matter across these systems.
HR-Font assigns correspondence and appearance to different representations: graphics descriptors align cross-script regions, while learned values carry the target family's visual character.

\paragraph{Font-bank content priors.}
CF-Font projects content into a linear space of corresponding glyph features from basis fonts, with blending weights determined by font-level similarity~\citep{wang2023cffont}.
This is the closest precedent for same-character bank fusion.
\method preserves individual donor residuals relative to one neutral anchor until local aggregation and separates structural proposals from target-reference appearance.
We aggregate anchor-relative residual candidates rather than fused absolute glyph features.
The result is a spatially adaptive variation prior that can use different donor evidence for different regions of the same glyph.

\paragraph{Geometric representation and decoupled conditioning.}
Signed distance representations support high-quality vector-font reconstruction and synthesis~\citep{xia2023vecfontsdf}; \method uses truncated signed distance fields as a normalized carrier for comparable glyph variation.
Decoupled image conditioning has also appeared outside font generation~\citep{ye2023ipadapter}.
HR-Font applies role-specific conditioning to cross-script typography: proposal geometry pairs with observed appearance in one generation system.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $c$ denote a target character and $x_c^0$ its rendering in a fixed neutral font.
For an unseen target font, the model observes an ordered reference set
$R=\{r_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$, where $1\leq n\leq 8$ during training and $n=8$ in the main evaluation.
The references and target may belong to different scripts.
A training bank $\Bset=\{B_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ contains fonts that render the target character and the reference character inventory.
The target font is excluded from the bank.

We seek a generator
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_c = G_\theta(x_t, x_c^0, \Dset_c(R), \Rset(R)),
  \label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is a noisy target image, $\Dset_c(R)$ is a set of candidate structural changes supported by the bank, and $\Rset(R)$ is appearance evidence extracted only from the observed target-font references.
This formulation supports multiple compatible designs while keeping character identity anchored to $x_c^0$.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Reference-conditioned donor proposals}
A frozen style encoder $E_s$ maps each reference to a global descriptor $h_i$.
Precomputed bank descriptors define a font-level similarity score $s_j(R)$.
We retrieve the top-$K$ donors and compute
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_j = \frac{\exp(s_j(R)/\tau)}{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal J(R)}\exp(s_\ell(R)/\tau)},
  \quad j\in\mathcal J(R), \quad |\mathcal J(R)|=K.
  \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
$\alpha$ ranks the retrieved structural proposals and enters spatial aggregation as a logit prior.
The main configuration uses $K=10$ and $\tau=0.07$; both values are frozen before formal evaluation.

\subsection{Set-Delta Variation Prior}
Let $\phi(I)$ contain a clipped signed distance field, spatial gradients, and an ink mask computed from glyph image $I$.
For every retrieved donor, we construct a same-character residual from the fixed neutral anchor:
\begin{equation}
  d_{j,c}=\mathcal N\!\left(\phi(B_{j,c})-\phi(x_c^0)\right),
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N$ performs scale and radius normalization and clips distances to eight pixels.
The common anchor represents each candidate as a change from neutral content rather than as absolute donor geometry.

An encoder maps each residual to multi-scale candidate features $e^s_{j,p}$ at spatial position $p$ and scale $s\in\{48,24\}$.
The current denoising and content state supplies queries $q^s_p$, while candidate geometry supplies keys $k^s_{j,p}$.
We retain the donor axis and aggregate separately at each position:
\begin{align}
 a^s_{j,p} &= \operatorname{softmax}_{j}\left(
 \frac{\langle q^s_p,k^s_{j,p}\rangle}{\sqrt d}
 +\lambda_\alpha\log(\alpha_j+\epsilon)\right), \\
 m^s_p &= \sum_{j\in\mathcal J(R)}a^s_{j,p}e^s_{j,p}.
 \label{eq:setdelta}
\end{align}
The main implementation maps $m^s$ to a displacement field that warps content features through the existing deformable path.
D7 evaluates residual-value injection as an alternative path.

The distinction from pre-averaged Delta is operational: a mean produces one global direction before seeing the target location, whereas Equation~\ref{eq:setdelta} permits different parts of one glyph to select different supported variations.

\subsection{Graphics-Informed Local Reference Attention}
Global style cues remain available as a sequence of per-reference pooled descriptors $\{h_i\}$ rather than their mean.
To retain local evidence, we additionally extract a $12\times12$ frozen intermediate map $F_i=E_s^{12}(r_i)$ and pool it to $4\times4$ tokens per reference.

For each local region $u$, we compute a graphics descriptor
\begin{equation}
 g_{i,u}=[\text{ink},\text{radius},\cos(2\vartheta),\sin(2\vartheta),
 \text{endpoint},\text{junction}]_{i,u}.
\end{equation}
The descriptor comes directly from each observed reference image and gives every local appearance value a reusable cross-script address.
Keys and values have deliberately different roles:
\begin{equation}
 k_{i,u}=W_k g_{i,u}, \qquad v_{i,u}=W_v F_{i,u}.
 \label{eq:kv}
\end{equation}
Queries derived from the target content and denoising state attend over all valid $(i,u)$ pairs.
Graphics primitives propose cross-script correspondence; learned features carry the target font's local appearance.
A padding mask supports variable $n$ and classifier-free guidance drops global references, local references, retrieval bias, and the Delta branch synchronously.

\subsection{Training objective and implementation boundary}
We inherit the FontDiffuser denoising backbone and its epsilon-prediction objective:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathbb E_{y,\epsilon,t}
  \left[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,x_c^0,\Dset_c(R),\Rset(R))\|_2^2\right].
\end{equation}
The total objective retains the existing perceptual reconstruction and deformation-offset regularizers,
$\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{perc}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{perc}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{off}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}$,
with the same coefficients in every matched arm.
The style and content encoders remain frozen and are consumed through versioned offline caches.
R1--R3 add no local style loss.
All new trainable projections use an initialization generator independent of the data and dropout random streams.
The full method operates from the requested reference set and requires no additional target-font glyph collection.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Questions and hypotheses}
The evaluation answers four preregistered questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does replacing legacy feature Delta with canonicalized geometry Delta reduce donor-appearance leakage (D2 versus D1 plus leakage probes)?
  \item Does preserving the donor axis improve compatible structure relative to geometry mean-Delta (D3 versus D2)?
  \item Do local reference values improve observable stroke attributes beyond per-reference global tokens (R2 versus R1)?
  \item Do graphics keys add useful cross-script correspondence beyond equal-capacity learned local attention (R3 versus R2, R4, and FSFont-style K/V)?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset and protocol}
The current corpus contains 260 fonts after a preregistered ink-coverage exclusion, split by font into 228 training, 16 validation, and 16 test fonts.
Protocol A renders $96\times96$ RGB glyphs using one font size per font, a neutral Noto Sans CJK Regular content face, 295 target characters, and a 338-character reference inventory.
All primary comparisons use the same split, ordered reference set, effective batch size, data order, classifier-free guidance draws, and F0@100k parent checkpoint.
The new method is trained with $n\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,8\}$ and evaluated primarily with eight references.
The exact data manifest hash, parent hash, optimizer, hardware, runtime, and total compute will be reported in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Baselines}
We prioritize faithful comparisons with FontDiffuser~\citep{yang2023fontdiffuser}, the direct cross-script methods FTransGAN~\citep{li2021ftransgan} and FCAGAN~\citep{zhao2024fcagan}, and the bank-based CF-Font~\citep{wang2023cffont}.
FSFont~\citep{tang2022fsfont} and VQ-Font~\citep{pan2023vqfont} are reported only under documented protocols compatible with the cross-script setting.
For every external baseline, a protocol card records its source and target scripts, reference budget, font-bank access, training corpus, and adaptation.
We separately report bank-enabled matched controls under identical HR-Font resources, exposing the value of each information source under equal conditions.
Internal controls include F0 (no RSI), F1 (official RSI source), F2 (old mean-Delta), D0--D6, and R0--R4.

\subsection{Metrics}
The primary endpoint is blind family/style preference, with confidence intervals clustered by font.
Identity accuracy and artifact/readability judgments define non-inferiority gates.
Geometry is measured with boundary Chamfer or SDF distance, hole and connected-component errors, endpoint error, skeleton-radius distributions, and slant/orientation distributions.
Local blind labels cover terminals, ligatures, outline treatment, weight contrast, and dry-brush texture.
Pixel metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, and L1) serve as diagnostics.
The E12 score provides a targeted, lineage-defined measure of family compatibility alongside human preference and geometry metrics.

We stratify every attribute as (i) visible in the references, (ii) unobserved but represented in the training bank, or (iii) unsupported by both.
This stratification measures observed-style transfer, bank-supported completion, and cases unsupported by either source.

\section{Scope and Future Opportunities}
HR-Font targets compatible completion rather than reproduction of a single hidden design.
Its structural vocabulary scales with the font bank and extends to rare, culturally specific, and emerging styles.
Vector control points and learned stroke graphs offer future extensions for finer endpoint and topology control.

The current platform operates on $96\times96$ rendered glyphs and focuses on Chinese-to-Western and neighboring-script transfer.
Extending the same evidence architecture to vector outlines, kerning, hinting, and text-line typography would support a broader cross-script type-design workflow.
Our evaluation combines blind human judgments, structure-aware measurements, and representative challenging cases to map the strongest opportunities for that expansion.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \method, an evidence architecture for cross-script font completion: bank-supported structural choice plus reference-driven appearance personalization.
\setdelta retains same-character, anchor-relative geometry candidates until spatial aggregation, while \graphicsref separates primitive-based correspondence keys from learned local style values.
Together, the two modules provide a scalable path from a few observed glyphs to a coherent cross-script family: \tbd{summarize headline result and strongest mechanism finding}.
